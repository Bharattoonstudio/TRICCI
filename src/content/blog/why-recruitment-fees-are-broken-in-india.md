# Why Recruitment Fees Are Broken in India (And How to Fix Them)

If you have ever hired through a recruitment agency in India, you have probably felt the sting: a fee that seems arbitrarily large, a consultant who disappears after placement, and no clear explanation of where your money actually went.

You are not imagining it. India's recruitment fee model is structurally broken — and it has been for decades.

## The Standard Model: Who Gets What

The typical Indian recruitment agency charges employers 8–12% of a candidate's annual CTC as a placement fee. On a ₹30 LPA hire, that is ₹2.4–3.6 lakhs per placement.

Here is what most employers do not know: the consultant who actually sourced, screened, and placed that candidate typically takes home 20–35% of that fee. The rest goes to the agency — for overhead, management layers, and profit.

So on a ₹3 lakh fee, the consultant who did the work earns ₹60,000–₹1,05,000. The agency keeps ₹1.95–2.4 lakhs for… what exactly?

## The Three Parties Who Lose

### Employers

You pay a premium fee but get no visibility into how it is structured. You cannot tell whether you are paying for genuine sourcing expertise or for a database query that any LinkedIn recruiter could run. Worse, most agencies charge the same fee regardless of the difficulty of the role or the quality of the hire.

### Independent Consultants

India has hundreds of thousands of independent recruitment consultants — people with deep domain expertise, strong networks, and years of experience placing candidates in niche roles. Most of them work through agencies that take 65–80% of every fee they generate. They have no leverage, no transparency, and no way to build equity in their own work.

### Candidates

In the traditional model, candidates are inventory. They are submitted to roles without their knowledge, their CVs are shared without consent, and they rarely receive feedback when they are rejected. The agency's incentive is to fill the role — not to find the right fit for the candidate.

## Why the Model Persists

The agency model persists because it solves a real coordination problem: employers do not want to manage dozens of individual consultants, and consultants do not want to handle billing, compliance, and client relationships on their own.

The agency sits in the middle and takes a large cut for providing that infrastructure.

The problem is not the coordination — it is the pricing. The infrastructure that agencies provide is worth 15–20% of the fee, not 65–80%.

## What a Fair Model Looks Like

A fair recruitment fee model has three properties:

**1. Transparent split.** Every party knows exactly how the fee is divided before the engagement begins. No hidden markups, no retroactive adjustments.

**2. Performance-based.** The fee is paid only when a hire is made and the candidate joins. No retainers, no search fees, no "shortlisting charges."

**3. Consultant-first.** The person who does the work — sourcing, screening, relationship management — should earn the majority of the fee. A 65–70% split to the consultant is fair. Anything below 50% is exploitative.

## The Ripple Effect of Fair Fees

When consultants earn more, they invest more in quality. They spend time understanding the role, building relationships with candidates, and providing honest feedback. They stop submitting CVs in bulk and start making targeted placements.

When employers pay transparent fees, they can evaluate the ROI of each hire. They can compare consultants on quality, not just speed. They stop treating recruitment as a black box.

When candidates are treated as people rather than inventory, they engage more honestly. They share their real motivations, their actual notice periods, their genuine salary expectations. Placements stick.

Transparency is not just an ethical choice. It is a better business model.

## What Employers Should Ask

Before signing with any recruitment agency or consultant, ask these questions:

- What percentage of the fee goes to the consultant who works on my role?
- Is the fee fixed or does it vary based on the hire?
- What happens if the candidate leaves within 90 days?
- Can I see a breakdown of what the fee covers?

If the agency cannot answer these questions clearly, that tells you everything you need to know about how they operate.

## Conclusion

India's recruitment industry is overdue for a reckoning. The technology exists to connect employers directly with independent consultants. The infrastructure exists to handle compliance, billing, and quality control without a 70% agency cut.
